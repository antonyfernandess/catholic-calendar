Rules for the Liturgical Calendar --------------------------------- The Liturgical Year every year begins with Advent. 1 => The Seasons of the Liturgical Year are as follows: i) Advent, ii) Christmas, iii) Ordinary Time, iv) Lent, v) Sacred Triduum, vi) Easter. 2 => The Liturgical Year is divided into two main cycles: the Temporal Cycle (Movables) and the Sanctoral Cycle. i) The Temporal Cycle focuses on the life of Christ and includes the seasons of Advent, Christmas, Lent, Sacred Triduum, Easter, and Ordinary Time. ii) The Sanctoral Cycle commemorates the lives of saints and includes feasts and memorials dedicated to them. 3 = > The Colour for the Liturgical Year is determined by the season or feast being celebrated. The main liturgical colors are: i) White: Used during the seasons of Christmas and Easter, Feasts and Solemnities of the Lord, the Blessed Virgin Mary, the angels, and saints who were not martyrs. ii) Green: Used during Ordinary Time. iii) Red: Used on Palm Sunday, Good Friday, Pentecost, for the feasts of martyrs and apostles, and for the celebrations of the Holy Cross and the Sacred Heart. iv) Rose: Only used on Gaudete Sunday (3rd Sunday of Advent) and Laetare Sunday (4th Sunday of Lent). v) Violet: Used during the seasons of Advent and Lent, and for the Sacrament of Reconciliation. 4 => The Season of Advent i) The first Sunday of Advent marks the beginning of the Liturgical Year and occurs four Sundays before Christmas. ii) Usually the first Sunday of Advent occurs between November 27 and December 3. iii) From the First Sunday four Sundays of Advent are counted, leading up to Christmas. iv) The Christmas is on 25th of December, and the season of Advent ends on the evening of December 24th. 5 => Christmastide
